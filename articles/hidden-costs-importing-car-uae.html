The Numbers

Hidden costs of importing a car into the UAE

A car that looks meaningfully cheaper abroad can stop being a deal once customs duty, VAT on top of that duty, shipping, GCC-spec conversion, and registration are all added up. Here's the full stack, not just the sticker price gap.

The purchase-price gap is what catches attention — a model that costs noticeably less overseas looks like an obvious saving. What actually lands in your driveway costs is the purchase price plus five or six additional layers, several of which are easy to underestimate until the bill for each one arrives separately.

The age limit that rules out a lot of "deals"

Before cost even enters the picture, Dubai and Abu Dhabi generally cap personal vehicle imports at 10 years old from the manufacture date — so heading into 2026, that means 2016-model-year or newer. A tempting older bargain found abroad may simply not be importable at all in these emirates, which makes this the first filter to apply before pricing anything else out.

Customs duty, then VAT on top of the duty

The UAE applies a flat 5% customs duty on the vehicle's CIF value — cost, insurance, and freight combined, not just the sticker price. Then 5% VAT applies on top of the CIF-plus-duty total, meaning the VAT is calculated on a number that already includes the duty. The two aren't additive in the simple sense of "10% of the car's price" — they compound on a base that already includes shipping and insurance, which pushes the real government-fee total above what a quick 5%+5% mental estimate suggests.

Shipping: the line with the widest range

International shipping commonly runs somewhere in the AED 3,000–15,000+ range, depending on the country of origin, whether the car travels via roll-on/roll-off or in a shared or dedicated container, and current freight market conditions. This is the single most variable line in the entire stack — worth getting an actual quote for the specific origin port rather than assuming a number.

GCC-spec compliance: modifications that aren't optional

A car built for a different market — US-spec vehicles are the common example — often doesn't meet UAE (ESMA) technical standards out of the box. Common required modifications include converting headlights to right-hand-traffic patterns, adding rear fog lights, and converting the speedometer to km/h. None of these are cosmetic preferences — they're compliance requirements for passing inspection, and each one is a real labor-and-parts cost on top of the car itself.

Testing, registration, and port handling

Once the car clears customs, it still needs ESMA compliance testing, then RTA (or the relevant local authority) roadworthiness testing and registration — commonly budgeted around AED 1,000–3,000 for plates and inspection — plus port handling and clearance fees typically in the AED 500–2,000 range. Neither of these is optional, and both apply regardless of how straightforward the car's compliance turns out to be.

Added together, importing typically adds somewhere in the range of 12–20% on top of the car's purchase price before it's even registered — before factoring in any GCC-spec conversion work a specific vehicle might need. On a car that looked, say, 15% cheaper abroad before shipping, that gap can shrink to very little, or disappear, once the full stack is priced in.

Run it with your own numbers: once the car lands, the 5-year total cost of ownership calculator shows what it actually costs to keep and run — insurance, fuel, and maintenance on top of everything above.

The actual question before importing

Not "is it cheaper abroad" but "is it still cheaper after duty, VAT-on-duty, shipping, any compliance conversion, and registration are all added to the overseas price." For a genuinely rare or significantly discounted vehicle, the answer can still be yes. For a modest price gap on a common model, the math very often erases the saving — and the only way to know which situation you're in is to actually run the full stack against a specific car and a specific shipping quote, not a rule of thumb.
