Owning a car in Dubai vs. Metro and Careem: the real monthly cost

"A car is expensive here" and "public transport isn't enough" are both partly true. Once fuel, insurance, Salik, and parking are amortized into a monthly figure and set against an actual NOL pass, the comparison is closer — and more situational — than either claim on its own.

Dubai's Metro and bus network covers a real share of the city, and ride-hailing fills a lot of the remaining gaps — but a car is still the default for a large share of residents. Whether that default is the cheaper option depends entirely on doing the monthly arithmetic rather than going on impression.

What ownership actually costs per month

The purchase price isn't the monthly cost — the running cost is a stack of recurring lines, several of which are easy to under-count because they don't arrive as a single visible bill.

LineTypical monthly range (AED)Why it's easy to miss
Fuel400–900Varies hugely with commute distance and traffic
Insurance (annual ÷ 12)200–500Paid once a year, so it doesn't feel monthly
Salik tolls150–450AED 4–6 per gate crossing depending on peak/off-peak timing, adds up fast on a two-gate daily commute
Parking (building/mall/paid zones)100–400Often several small charges rather than one bill
Maintenance & tyres (annual ÷ 12)150–350Lumpy — a AED 0 month followed by a large service bill

Stacked together, a realistic all-in range for a mid-size sedan commuting daily across a couple of toll gates lands somewhere around AED 1,000–2,600 a month, before financing. Add a car loan or lease payment on top and the number moves meaningfully higher — this table intentionally excludes financing since it varies so much by vehicle and terms.

What Metro plus Careem actually costs per month

A Nol Silver card's unlimited monthly pass currently runs roughly AED 140 for a single zone, up to roughly AED 350 for all zones — a Gold-class pass roughly doubles those figures. For most commuters not crossing the full breadth of the city daily, a one- or two-zone pass covers the core commute. The gap Metro doesn't cover well — the first/last mile, off-hours trips, or destinations away from a station — typically gets filled with ride-hailing, at a commonly cited base fare plus a per-kilometer rate that can add up to a meaningful sum if used daily rather than occasionally.

LineTypical monthly range (AED)
Nol monthly pass (1–2 zones)140–230
Careem/taxi for gap trips (occasional use)150–500

Put together, a Metro-plus-occasional-Careem commuter typically lands somewhere around AED 300–730 a month — meaningfully below the low end of car ownership, as long as the gap trips stay occasional rather than becoming the daily default, which is where the ride-hailing side of the comparison can quietly balloon.

What ownership buys that the transit stack doesn't

The cost gap is real, but it isn't the whole decision. A car buys point-to-point flexibility, a fixed schedule that doesn't depend on station coverage or peak-hour ride-hailing surge pricing, and the ability to carry things a commute on foot or transit can't. For irregular schedules, locations away from Metro coverage, or households needing to move people and cargo together, that flexibility has real value that doesn't show up in a pure cost table.

The actual question worth asking

Not "which is cheaper in general" but "how much is my specific commute pattern actually going to cost each way, and what is the flexibility genuinely worth to me." Someone living and working near Metro stations with a predictable schedule is looking at a very different number than someone whose commute or work pattern doesn't line up with the rail network at all. Running both stacks with real, specific numbers — not the illustrative ranges above — is the only way to get an answer that actually applies.
